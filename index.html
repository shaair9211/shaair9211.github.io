Effects of Illegal On-Street Parking on Signalized Intersection Capacity

Authors: Dr. Ashar Ahmed, Muhammad Shair · Venue: CSCE '24

A field study examining how illegal on-street parking reduces effective roadway capacity at signalized intersections, using observed data to quantify the impact on throughput and delay.

Real-time Signal Optimization Using Machine Learning and Computer Vision

Authors: Dr. Ashar Ahmed, Muhammad Shair · Venue: European Transport, Waiting for acceptance

A real-time vehicle detection model paired with a machine learning-based signal optimization system, tested at a Karachi intersection under undisciplined traffic conditions. Evaluating whether data-driven methods can practically improve signal timing in real-world, non-lane-disciplined traffic.

05/10

 

Off the critical path

Beyond Traffic ·  Hardware, on weekends

Besides all the transportation stuff, you'll find me surrounded by jumper wires and breadboards and around my half finished projects. The one build worth mentioning is a small line-following robot, first wired up on an Arduino Uno, later rebuilt around an ESP32 and rewritten in MicroPython. Nothing revolutionary, I know, but still, worth mentioning ig.

Not every project made it to the finish line (most of them are failures, to be fair), but that's just part of the journey, right? Along the way I've also experimented with energy metering using the PZEM-004T, a Bluetooth-controlled RC car, an RC plane, and a 3-axis camera mount controller and many others I don't even remember. Some worked, some didn't, but every one of them taught me something new.


Most of my hardware experience is with Arduino and ESP32. One other device that's been on my wishlist for a while now is the NVIDIA Jetson Nano, expensive, but I'll just get it soon, InShaAllah (hopefully). Then, next up will be a robotic limb, which is why I've been getting comfortable with CoppeliaSim.

And this is just a hobby. It doesn't mean I'm good at it or anything, no. I still mess up constantly. But I keep coming back to it anyway, because well... sighs, I love doing it.
